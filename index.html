    The city

    Location, size, and a short historical sketch — founded 315 BC, Roman, Byzantine, Ottoman until 1912, burnt in 1917, remade by the 1922 population exchange. Then say what kind of city Thessaloniki is now, and give the evidence rather than asserting it.

    Say which population figure you are using and why. The municipality, the metropolitan area and the regional unit are three different numbers and most reports mix them.

    What the city is dealing with

    The challenges behind the cultural and placemaking programmes: a long-argued "arrested development" relative to Athens, the working port at the city's edge, transport that only got fixed in 2024, short-let pressure on the centre, and a persistent question about whether being second city is a constraint or a position. Two or three of these, with the structural cause rather than the symptom.

    Where the desk and the street disagree

    The most useful paragraph on this page. Name one thing the statistics or the city's own communications claim, and what you found when you went to look — a 1997 venue that is closed, a regeneration plan with nothing on the ground, a district whose reputation does not match a Tuesday morning. This is the honest answer to the criterion about mixing desk and field research.
